# Unicorn-Companies-Analysis
For this project, we analyze a dataset of global unicorn ompanies that have achieved a valuation of $1 billion or more. The main goal was to uncover patterns around when these companies were founded and how long it took them to reach unicorn status.

We began by loading the data and conducting an initial inspection of its shape, data types, and first few records. The dataset comprises 1,074 entries across 10 features, covering details like company valuation, market sector, location, and investors. First step was converting the date joined column from a  string object into a datetime format, which allowed us to pull out specific attributes like years, months, and week numbers.  

Our exploratory analysis yielded several key findings. Looking at the distribution of founding years, we found that most unicorns in the dataset originated between 2012 and 2018, with 2015 marking the single highest peak at 155 companies. We then computed a new metric "years needed to join" by subtracting the founding year from the year unicorn status was achieved. I examined specific year 2020. I was interested how fast companies become unicorns during covid-time. Interestingly, for companies founded in 2020, growth was remarkably fast: 21 of the 25 startups became unicorns within their very first year. A boxplot across months was used to map out these growth windows further.We isolated the 2020 cohort and extracted ISO week numbers to see if additions clustered at specific points in the year. Plotting these weekly counts on a bar graph highlighted clear surges during the final weeks of the year, particularly around weeks 49 and 50.

Lastly, i tried to get info about how much years companies needed to become unicorn based on their found year. As our study found, companies founded more recently are becoming unicorn companies much faster than other companies.
